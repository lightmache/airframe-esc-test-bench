# Engineering Test Log: ESC Integration & Power System Diagnostics
**Date:** June 23, 2026  
**Hardware Platform:** STM32F3 Discovery (Cortex-M4) & Arduino Uno  
**Subsystem:** Propulsion / Electronic Speed Controller (ESC)  

## 1. Objective
Establish a reliable, software-dampened automated throttle ramping profile on the STM32 platform using hardware timers to control a high-voltage brushless propulsion system, transitioning from low-fidelity Arduino prototyping code.

---

## 2. Test Configurations & Timeline

### Phase 1: Microcontroller Transition & Clock Drift
* **Action:** Attempted to port a basic Arduino looping ramp sequence to the STM32 on pin `PA8`.
* **Observations:** The ESC consistently experienced initialization cut-offs or failed to complete its "positive-sounding" startup melody. 
* **Analysis:** Identified architectural differences in how the underlying `Servo.h` libraries handle microsecond timing across different clock speeds (16 MHz vs. 72 MHz+), cutting off the ESC's mandatory calibration window.
* **Resolution:** Modified the script to force a dedicated, flat $1000\mu s$ baseline hold for 5 full seconds immediately upon bootup to allow initialization self-checks to clear safely.

### Phase 2: Hardware Lockout & OpenOCD Failures
* **Action:** Shifted physical connections along the dual-row headers (`PA8` / `PC8` / `GND`).
* **Observations:** Arduino IDE threw a compilation/upload failure: `Error: open failed ** OpenOCD init failed **`.
* **Analysis:** High current demands or physical pin shifting caused a momentary logic rail voltage drop, forcing the onboard ST-LINK debugger MCU into hardware protection mode.
* **Resolution:** Performed a cold USB power cycle, isolated the board from the propulsion battery, and forced a fresh USB enumeration instance on a separate port to restore debugger communication.

### Phase 3: Battery Sag & Voltage Protection Lockout
* **Action:** Re-tested the classic script on both Arduino (Pin 9) and STM32 (`PD15`), capping peak throttle at a safe bench limit of $1250\mu s$.
* **Observations:** The motor would spin for a fraction of a second and immediately shut down. The issue persisted across both microcontrollers.
* **Analysis:** The supply source—a nominal 20V battery pack—had run down to 14V. Under immediate motor acceleration, the voltage sag dropped the pack below critical thresholds ($< 3.0\text{V}$ per cell for a 5S configuration). This actively tripped the ESC’s internal **Low-Voltage Cutoff (LVC)** protection scheme. Additionally, the 3.3V logic signal of the STM32 faced lower noise immunity against electromagnetic interference (EMI) generated by the over-voltage hardware environment compared to the Arduino's 5V logic rail.

## 4. Next Steps & Action Items
1. **Battery Remediation:** Place the 20V battery pack on a balanced charge cycle to restore nominal cell voltages and eliminate under-voltage protection tripping.
2. **Signal Shielding:** Braiding/twisting the White (Signal) and Black (GND) ESC wires together to increase noise rejection against high-voltage EMI on the 3.3V logic rail.
3. **Validation Run:** Re-flash the dampening firmware to verify a smooth 5-second acceleration glide once stable input power is re-established.
